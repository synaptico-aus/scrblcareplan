name: Assessment evidence sources
description: Keep AI notes and original transcript evidence distinct without dropping either
---

When an assessment contains both AI-written notes and original transcript text, retain both and clearly distinguish their evidence provenance. Do not silently discard the notes or represent them as verbatim participant speech.

**Why:** The user explicitly chose both sections, clearly distinguished, after a controlled reproduction failed on a summary-heavy first part. Source provenance does not itself establish clinical relevance or guarantee generation success.

**How to apply:** Carry source labels through drafting, citation repair, saved drafts and exports. Preserve exact excerpt checks, uncertainty and clinical review. Do not guess the source type of ambiguous input or use summary text to infer consent.

Use first person for non-goal care narratives, without routine "The notes report/say" or "I said" framing. Goals may use third person or "my provider". Keep source attribution in the evidence appendix and review notice; explicitly distinguish conflicting accounts when needed for safety.

**Why:** The user asked to restore first-person writing after source attribution made ordinary care instructions read as repetitive reports. Narrative voice must not imply that secondary notes are verbatim speech or proof of consent.

**How to apply:** Apply this voice to newly generated drafts from both notes and transcripts, leaving exact evidence excerpts unchanged. Do not silently rewrite saved plans, invent agreement, or hide contradictions to achieve a smoother narrative.
